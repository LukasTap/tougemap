The join
Putting both nights on one axis means eliding 14 hours of daylight. How that elision is drawn decides whether the chart tells the truth.
Continuous + marker rule
The marked segment is a slope the data never had. It reads as fog clearing over one step — it is 14 hours of daylight that are not on this chart. The rule does not stop the eye tracing through it.
Severed + rule in the gap
Nothing crosses the gap, so nothing can be read across it. Each night keeps its own shape and the second one stays dimmed, so tonight still reads first.
Hour spacing stays identical in both halves, so a slope means the same thing wherever it appears. Check the map at 04:00 and tonight is a narrow stub with tomorrow filling the rest — which is the honest picture of your position at 04:00.
Cost
Each night gets half the width it had as its own chart — 14.8px per hour instead of 30.8. Plenty for a line; it is the reason the per-hour numbers were never coming back.
